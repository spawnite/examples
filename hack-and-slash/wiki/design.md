# Bladebound

A 2D hack and slash with MMO features: the hero hunts monsters for experience, levels up and spends stat points. The hero is a modular sprite the player dresses at character creation (face, hair, and sliders for hair, skin and each eye's colour), and the sprite shows the helmet or hat and the weapon equipped: a bow, or a sword and shield, to start.

## Inspiration

- [MapleStory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MapleStory): side-scrolling maps full of respawning monsters that the player hunts for experience and drops. There is no winning a round: the goal is the next level, and dying costs a slice of experience and sends the hero back to town. **Take:** points to spend on strength, dexterity, intelligence and luck on each level up.
- [Dungeon Fighter Online](https://www.techshout.com/games-like-maplestory/): a side-scrolling beat 'em up, where the player clears rooms of enemies and then fights the dungeon's boss. A run is won on the boss and lost at zero health. **Take:** combos that juggle crowds, so slashing feels weighty.
- Realm of the Mad God: a top-down shooter where shots fly in every direction and loot drops in bags. A run ends for good when the hero dies. **Take:** loot that shows on the hero the moment it is equipped.

## What game

"Hunt monsters in the wilds, level up and spend stat points on the hero I made."

- **Won:** never. The hunt has no end: the goal is the next level.
- **Lost:** at zero health the hero wakes in town and loses some experience. The level is never lost.
- **Length:** open-ended. The hero's level and experience are saved in the browser between visits, as an MMO keeps a character.

Built: the game is named in `index.html`, `<Game name>` and the lobby, which states the rules. "Enter the wilds", in the town's menu, goes to the run. The coin readout top right now reads `Level 1 · XP 0 / 20` from the hero's progress store in `src/hero/progress.ts`, saved to the browser's storage. The template's goal ring and ball are gone. The run still holds the template's coins until step 2 brings the monsters.

## First interactions

The creator: "a hack and slash where you hit an arc towards the mouse cursor, while bow shoots it towards that route. Holding the key should be possible but it would be the same logic but repeating it." Then, after playing it: items and drops from the monsters, monsters that stay in their spawn areas, the stats in one window rather than half the screen, an alert for unallocated stat points that opens the stat window, a slime attack you can see coming, and a sword arc that shows on slopes.

- **Attacking:** a click, or the J key, attacks toward the pointer; holding either repeats the attack at the hero's attack speed. The sword cuts an arc 130° wide and 2.1 m deep, hitting every monster in it; the bow looses an arrow along the same line, 13 m, stopping in the first monster. Hits deal the weapon's damage give or take 15%, flash the monster white and knock it back, and show the number over it. The arc is drawn at the hero's own height and over the ground, and the pointer is cast at her height, so both work on a slope.
- **Monsters:** Moss Slimes (level 1) in two meadows either side of the start, Ember Slimes (level 3) in a patch to the north. The creator asked for two tempers. **Passive** (Moss Slimes, white names) roam their area and fight only once hit. **Aggressive** (Ember Slimes, red names) turn on a hero who comes within their aggro range (5.5 m). Either kind, angered, shows a red "!" and chases until it is twice its aggro range from where the chase began, then walks back to its spawn point, ignoring the hero on the way unless she hits it, and heals to full there. Beside her it winds up a lunge for 0.4 s, crouching and shaking with its fangs bared over a red ring, then leaps; the lunge lands if she is still within a metre, and a hit during the wind-up knocks it out of the lunge. A slain monster respawns in its area 6 s later.
- **Bosses:** the creator asked for a boss that keeps killing in one area wakes: higher level and stronger, with a dash and AoE attacks of different shapes, warned for 1 to 5 seconds, dealing damage in proportion to the warning. Each moss meadow counts its kills and wakes the **Moss King** (level 5, 420 HP) at 12; the ember patch wakes the **Ember Tyrant** (level 9, 1100 HP) at 10. A count at the top of the screen shows the area's progress, and a banner announces the waking. A boss is a crowned slime with a gold nameplate and a health bar across the top of the screen; aggressive, never knocked back, leashed and healed like the rest, and it drops a guaranteed haul. Every 2.4 to 4 seconds (faster below half health) it uses a skill, each drawn on the ground as a red shape that fills over its warning and then goes off, hurting the hero if she still stands in it:
    - **Dash:** a bar toward her, warned for 1 s, then it charges along it (24 / 40 damage).
    - **Slam:** a 3.2 m circle under where she stood, 1.5 s.
    - **Cleave:** a 100° wedge 7 m long toward her, 1.2 s.
    - **Ripple:** a ring from just outside the boss to 8 m, 2.5 s: safe beside it.
    - **Cross** (the Tyrant): four 11 m bars, one at her, 3 s.
    - **Cataclysm** (the Tyrant, below half health): a 9.5 m circle round it, 5 s: time to run.
      An area attack deals the boss's damage a second (10 for the King, 16 for the Tyrant) for each second of warning. A slain boss's pending attacks go with it, and its area's count starts again.
- **Rewards:** each kill gives experience (a level up gives 5 stat points and full health, shows a banner and opens the stat window) and drops loot that pops out: gold, Red Potions and gear by each kind's drop table. Walking over loot takes it.
- **Items:** swords (Rusty, Iron, Ember Blade), bows (Short, Hunter's, Ember Longbow), shields (Wooden Buckler, Iron Kite) and headgear (Leather Cap, Iron Helm, Feathered Hat), each with damage, armor, health or stat bonuses. The starter kit is the Rusty Sword and Wooden Buckler worn, a Short Bow and 3 potions carried. A bow takes both hands, so it puts the shield in the bag. Q drinks a potion (50 HP).
- **Windows:** the bag (I) shows what she wears in a column (head, weapon, shield) beside a 5 × 4 grid of what she carries, each item its own pixel sprite edged in its rarity's colour, a stack with its count, and her gold. A mouse hover shows a tooltip at the cursor with the item's name, rarity, kind and bonuses; a tap or a click pins the tooltip over the item with its Equip, Drink or Take off button; a double-click uses it. The window keeps its size whatever is looked at. The stat window (C) is one small pane: each stat, what it does, a + while points are left, and her damage, speed, armor and health. An alert at the top of the screen stands while points are unspent and opens it. A quick bar along the bottom holds the potion, bag and stat buttons. The skills window (K) shows her trees, a node for each skill with its rank, and what the chosen one does now and at its next rank, with a button to learn it.
- **The end:** at 0 HP the world pauses on "You fell in battle", which names the experience lost (10% of the level's); "Wake in town" goes to the lobby. Level, stats, gold, bag and gear persist.

Built: the fight is the `combat` plugin in `src/combat/systems.ts`, whose systems run after the engine's rules, listed in `src/game.ts`, reading the pointer and keys from `src/combat/aim.ts`. Monsters are engine `Entity`s with `Health`, `Chase` and the game's `Monster` trait (`src/monsters/`), spawned per area by `Spawner`. Items and drop tables are data in `src/items/items.ts`, and every sprite, slime, loot and effect alike, is pixel art painted on a canvas at runtime (`src/sprites/canvas.ts`, `src/items/icons.ts`, `src/monsters/slimeSprite.ts`). The HUD is `src/hud/HeroHud.tsx`.

Proved in the playtest for the bosses: 12 kills in the east meadow woke the Moss King in 25 s, with its banner, nameplate and top bar; its cleave, ripple and slam drawn and filling on the ground; a ripple missing her in its safe centre; a cleave hitting her for 12 and two slams for 15 each; and her falling to it in about 12 s of standing still. Not proved: the dash, the Ember Tyrant, the cross and the cataclysm.

Proved in the playtest: a slime's wind-up (angry face, fangs, red ring); held J killing four slimes toward the pointer, reaching level 2 with the stat window opening on 5 points; walking over loot taking gold, a potion and a Wooden Buckler; the alert opening the stat window; points spent into DEX; the Short Bow equipped and its arrows killing a slime at range; the defeat among the Ember Slimes, pausing on its dialog; and a second visit starting clean at full health with fresh monsters. Not proved: a single click through the playtest tool, which never reaches the game while the playtest holds the world, though the creator's clicks do in the preview.

## Character

The creator: modular generated sprites, a face and hair chosen at character creation, colour sliders for hair, skin and eyes with each eye its own colour, and the helmet or hat and the weapon equipped drawn on the hero. First built as a hand-painted chibi sprite; the creator then asked for their model, `baseline.zip` (a Meshy chibi with pink twin tails and a cat-ear headset, with a running animation), as the hero's baseline, drawn as pixel art like the world.

- **The hero:** the creator's model, 1.27 m tall with her head at 0.62 of its modelled size (1.5 m at its own), drawn with three bands of toon light and, at the creator's word, with no pixel filter: the world's canvas is drawn small and scaled up, so she has a canvas of her own laid over it at the page's full resolution, drawn each frame through the world's camera where her body stands. She therefore draws in front of everything in the world. (Before that she was drawn as a sprite at the slimes' pixel size, which the creator asked for and then had removed.) She runs with the model's own run, played at her walking pace so her feet keep up; she stands on the run's first frame, where her feet are closest, since the file has no idle. She faces where she attacks, else where she walks.
- **Recolouring:** the model's texture is an atlas of flat patches, sampled texel by texel, so a shader sorts each texel by its colour and where it sits on her in the bind pose, and gives hair, skin, iris and outfit texels the chosen colour at their own brightness. An iris must also lie in the eyes' own corner of the texture, which no hair uses, and each eye is the side of her face it sits on, so her left and right eyes take their own colours.
- **Creation:** on the first visit the town shows her close up beside the creator: twin tails or short hair (the short cut drops every vertex her head bone carries below her crown or past its sides, but not the headset's near-black cups and band), and sliders along colour ramps for hair, skin, eyes and outfit. The eyes share one slider until "Same colour for both eyes" is switched off. Random picks a look; Done keeps it; "Change look" in the town's menu reopens it, with the HUD hidden as it is the first time. The look is saved with the hero; a look saved for the old sprite maps its long styles to twin tails and its short ones to the short cut.
- **Gear on the hero:** headgear, the sword or bow and the shield are built from shapes in each item's colours and hung on her head, right hand and left forearm bones in the bind pose, so they move with her run. A cap, helm or hat hides the headset's cat ears. A sword swing, 0.24 s, holds the blade level and sweeps it from her right, across her front, to her left, her arm and chest turning with it; a shot holds the bow out ahead, upright. A bow takes both hands, so the shield is not worn with it.
- **Where she starts:** the town, then the wilds at the south of the clearing, facing the moss slimes either side.

Built: `public/models/hero/base-running.glb` holds the model, loaded with three's `GLTFLoader`: the creator's bald base with its run, cut from 106,000 triangles to 60,000 with its corners shared, so she is skinned at 30,000 points a frame instead of 317,000 and looks the same (she is skinned every frame and drawn twice, once for her shadow). `src/hero/modelRig.ts` clones the model, recolours it and builds and hangs the gear; `src/hero/HeroModel.tsx`, the Player's overlay, hides the engine's capsule, faces her, blends the run and the stand, poses the attacks, and draws her on her own full-resolution canvas over the world's. The creator is `src/hud/CharacterCreator.tsx`, the look `src/hero/look.ts`.

The creator's notes after playing it, each fixed: the eye colour tinted fringe hair over the eyes; the short cut holed the headset; the hero was finer than the slimes' pixels; and the sword did not swing properly. Then: "remove the pixel filter for the character".

Proved in the playtest: the model standing in town in its own colours; a blue right eye and a gold left one with the fringe over them untinted, and the short cut with the headset whole; the hero as a sprite at the slimes' pixel size, edged in dark; the swing's blade forward-left mid-cut and at her left at its end, drawn at fine pixels to see it; short hair, green hair, a blue outfit and a blue right eye with a gold left one set with the creator, the tails cut clean; the model running in the wilds; and a sword swing toward the pointer with the slime hit. Proved in a model viewer: the Leather Cap, the Feathered Hat with short hair and the Iron Helm on her head, the shield on her forearm, and the swing's start and end. Not proved in the game: the bow's pose, and the helm and hat, which drop only from Ember Slimes.

**A smaller head.** The creator asked for a smaller head, to move away from the chibi look. Her head is drawn at 0.62 of the model's size, shrunk about its bone at her jaw each frame after her animation, so her face, wig and headgear, which hang on that bone, shrink with it, and every fit on her head keeps its measure (`headSize` in `src/hero/modelRig.ts`). She stands 1.27 m to her crown where she stood 1.5 m, and the townspeople, drawn from her model, shrink the same. Her body keeps the model's short limbs: proportions further from a chibi's take a new base model, with every armor set fitted to it again. The bars over her head follow her head's size. Proved in screenshots of the character creator and the town, the short cut and the twin tails, at sizes from 0.75 to 0.48: the creator asked for it smaller still, found 0.48 too small, and 0.58 a little small.

**The bald base (latest).** The creator then gave a bald base character, running (`base_character_running.fbx`), "so that the hairstyles can fit without changing the head", and four wigs made in Meshy (short, ponytail, curly, twin curls) as Blender files. The hero is now that base on its Mixamo rig: her skin, outfit and boots are coloured by the bones each part of her hangs from. Her face is painted on a patch shaped to the front of her head like a decal (each point a hair's breadth off her skin), with big chibi eyes, and eight faces to pick: calm, smile, fierce, blush, sleepy, wink, surprised and cat, each eye its own colour and her brows in her hair's. The wigs are read straight out of the Blender files (no Blender is installed: a small reader pulls their meshes out) into `public/models/hair/*.bin`, turned Y-up and fitted to her head, full and standing well off it as a chibi's hair does; under a wig her scalp takes the hair's colour, so no gap shows her bald head. Her face, wig, hat and shield follow the skin round their bones by the skinning's own matrix, so they turn with her head and arms however the run turns their bones. The creator's notes on the first fit, each fixed: the face was on the back of her head; the hair was too small against the old twin tails; and the face too small against the old one.

**Armor.** The creator gave four armor sets made in Meshy (Arcane Robes, Battle Mage, Berserker and Knight), each a whole bald chibi wearing it with no rig, and asked for each set's gloves and boots apart from its armor. Each set is fitted to her bind shape (by her body rather than her head, since the sets' heads are bigger), its limbs turned a few degrees to meet hers, its head and bare skin cut away, and cut at the elbow and the knee into a body piece, gloves and boots; each piece is skinned from her nearest point, so it bends where she bends, and sits at least 7 mm off her skin. Under a piece she wears, her own skin draws a centimetre in, so no running limb pokes through. They are twelve items with three new slots, Body, Gloves and Boots: the robes drop from moss slimes, the Battle Mage's from ember slimes, and the Berserker's and the Knight's from the bosses. The bag's worn places are two across, with each empty one showing a faint outline of what it takes.

**Moves.** The creator then gave the Berserker and the Arcane Robes rigged, with animations: a sword slash, a parry, a fighting walk, a walk and a run, and two idles. Their rigs are hers bone for bone, but each body's joints sit a little differently, so each move is kept as how far each bone turns from its bind pose and fitted to hers as she loads (`src/hero/moves.ts`, from `public/models/hero/moves.json`). Every sword attack plays the slash's cut, from the top of its wind-up to the blade at her left, fast enough that the blade crosses her front about a tenth of a second after the attack, and faster as she attacks faster; standing it is her whole body, moving only above her hips, so her legs keep running. A hit on her sword plays the parry; a slow walk walks, and a fighting walk in a fight; she breathes as she stands and stretches after seven seconds still. The sword is held in her fist and turns with her hand; the bow keeps its drawn pose. The creator's notes on the first moves, each fixed: she looked down, so her head is lifted each frame to no more than a little below level; and the sword was not in her hand, but at her wrist and still. The creator asked for the hair smaller again: the wigs stand between their first fit and the larger one.

**Graphics.** The creator found the game slow on their phone and asked for less detail there. Measured on the laptop's real GPU, most of a frame was the meadow's decorative grass tufts: ten million vertices a frame, seen or not, against the hero's few hundred thousand. The menu now steps a graphics level, Auto, High, Medium, Low and Minimum, and names the level alone; the engine's performance overlay shows the frames a second. The creator then asked for Auto alone, adjusting as the frames drop: Auto starts a phone at Medium and a computer at High, and drei's PerformanceMonitor, from three seconds into a scene, judges each two and a half seconds of frames against the screen's rate (on a 60 Hz screen: under 45 a second draws less, holding 58 draws more). A level found too slow is tried again only after 30 seconds of keeping up, twice as long each time it fails again, so a device on its edge settles; the level Auto settles on is kept for the next visit, and a level the player held before is set back to Auto once. Proved in a phone-shaped window with the CPU slowed twentyfold: High to Minimum in 18 seconds, still drawing, and back to High within half a minute of freeing it.

The engine sets the canvas and the shadow for a chosen level; the game sets the canvas on Auto, and the rest itself (`src/view/graphics.tsx`).

**The fight, retuned.** The creator found her movement and swing "weirdly fast": she ran at 5 m/s, her run drawn for 2.6 and played at twice that, and her sword swung more than twice a second with its slash quickened by a third. She runs at 3.6 m/s now, and the sword swings 1.4 times a second before AGI at the slash's own pace, each swing hitting as the two it replaced did together, so her damage a second holds. The creator then asked for a fifth attribute, Critical, raising the critical rate and critical damage, and no dodge rate: a hit is critical at 5% and 1% a point, at most 60%, and deals 150% and 3% a point; a critical number stands larger, orange, with a "!". The Berserker's and the Battle Mage's gloves carry a little of it.

**Dodging.** The creator asked for a swipe to dodge on a phone held upright, and a dodge button otherwise: a flick of at least 40 pixels, lifted within 300 ms, dodges the way it flicked, up the screen being the way the camera looks; on a wide screen a Dodge button beside the potion, and Space on a keyboard, dodge the way she walks, else the way she faces. A dodge carries her 3.5 m in 0.3 s, leaning into it with dust at her feet; nothing hurts her through it ("Dodge" rises where a hit would have landed), she cannot attack mid-dodge, and she can dodge again 0.9 s after the last began. Proved on an emulated phone with real touch swipes each way, a slow drag still walking, and on a wide screen with Space and the button.

**Twin tails and face plates.** The creator gave a twin-tail hairstyle and a sheet of face plates made in Meshy, and asked for the ponytail gone ("it sucks"). The twin tails, 390,000 triangles, were welded and cut to 9,000 (`scripts/wig-build.html`) and take the ponytail's place in the list, so a hero who wore it wears them. The sheet held four painted faces (a closed-eyed smile, a grin with whisker marks, a gasp, a wink), a cat-ear headset and a bob. Each face was photographed straight on (`scripts/face-bake.html`) and its skin cut away (`scripts/bake-faces.py`, to `public/models/faces/`); they followed the eight painted faces as Joy, Grin, Gasp and Sly, their brows in her hair's colour and Sly's open eye in her right eye's. The creator's notes on the first plates, each fixed: the faces were "weirdly shaped" against the plates, their features laid by the painted faces' eye spacing and so shrunk to the middle of her face, so a plate is now laid on a patch of its own over most of the front of her head, its photograph projected from the front as it was taken, the plate's cheeks across her head's width and its chin at hers; and her forehead was "coloured". That was her hair's colour: under a wig her scalp is tinted it, so no gap shows her bald head, and the tint's edge ran across her forehead, which the short cut's thick bangs hid and the twin tails' thin ones did not, so her hair's colour blended into her skin there. Over her brow the hairline now rises to where every wig's cap sits. (Her head, and both faces, are also lit mostly flat now, the rest of her keeping its bands, as the toon light's darkest band had been the first suspect.) The creator then asked for the twin curls larger (now a quarter larger) and found the bob "not correct looking": it is a long cut rather than a short one, and it now sits lower and further back, its fringe at her brow. They had asked for the headset and the bob as well (`scripts/sheet-build.html`): the bob, 8,000 triangles, is a sixth hairstyle, turned a quarter, as the sheet shows it from the side; the headset, 4,600 triangles with its colours sampled from its texture into its corners, is the Cat-Ear Headset, a rare head item (+1 armor, +2 AGI, +2 CRI) that the bosses drop, one put in every bag to try on; its cat's ears stand out of her hair and its cups on her ears. Both were fitted by eye. The creator then found the headset "small and parts of it got clipped", and the front of the bob too. Both had been cut from the sheet by a straight line that fell inside them, keeping only what lay right of x 0.25 when each starts at about 0: the headset lost most of one cup and a side of its band, the bob a third of itself, its fringe among it, and the quarter turn had only made up for the missing piece. The sheet is six pieces apart, the four plates and the two parts, so each part is now taken whole. The headset, 4,050 triangles, is turned level (it lay 13° askew), set about its cups' middle, and its neckband, which loops behind the neck at the cups' height and would float behind her head, left out; it is worn 10% larger, its band leaning back 20° to stand over her crown, over every hairstyle. The bob, 8,000 triangles, faces forward as the headset does, with no turn, at 0.8 of the sheet's size, its crown just over her head and its fringe at her brow.

**Painted faces hidden.** The creator found the eight painted faces "not scaled properly" beside the plates and asked to hide them for now. The creator offers only the four plates, Joy, Grin, Gasp and Sly; a hero wearing a painted face wears Joy. The painted faces stay in the code, and a look still keeps its place among every face, so they can come back.

## Camera

The creator first asked for the world in pixel art and a 3/4 top-down camera, then for a more dynamic camera, and then: "I kinda wanted to be able to rotate it to make the game 3d instead of 2d". Asked, they chose an MMO free orbit, and full-resolution 3D rather than the pixel art.

- **The camera:** it follows her, starting 10 m back and 50° from straight up, looking north. The right mouse button (or the middle) orbits it round her and tilts it, between 20° and 82° from straight up; the wheel zooms it from 4 m to 18 m. On a phone, two fingers put down together turn it as they drag and zoom it as they pinch. The left button and one finger stay the attack's and the walk's. Walking goes the way the camera faces. It stays where the player leaves it, and pulls in front of a trunk that stands between it and her.
- **Moving with the fight:** it leads a quarter of the way toward the pointer, up to 1.6 m, easing after it; it no longer leads ahead of her walk, which the creator asked to see only in a dash, so during a dodge it leans 1.2 m the way she dodges and eases back after; pulls out 6% while she runs; shakes a little on each hit she lands, more on a kill and most when she is hurt; and punches in on a kill and more on a level up. The lead follows whichever way the camera faces.
- **The look:** full resolution, the world as the engine draws it; the engine's quality level sets how sharp on a weak device. The hero is drawn in the world again, so a tree can hide her, and after the fight's effects, so a sword's arc never covers her. The slimes and the loot stay pixel-art sprites that face the camera.
- **The town:** the same camera, close and nearly level (4.2 m, 78°), turned round her with the right button or two fingers to see every side. Upright on a phone, it stands back and lifts her above the creator.

Built: `src/view/OrbitCamera.tsx` is the engine's following camera with the buttons moved, framed once its orbit mounts; its lead, zoom and shake slide and scale the lens's window, so the follow and the aim stay exact. `src/combat/TouchControls.tsx` reads two fingers into camera-controls' turn and dolly. The pixel view and the pixel camera are gone.

Proved in the playtest: the wilds' start framed over her with both meadows in view; a right-drag turning the view; W walking her into the screen in the turned view; a left click swinging toward the pointer there and hitting three slimes, with her in front of the arc; and the town in full-resolution 3D with her shadow on the ground. Not proved: the two-finger turn and pinch, and the wheel's zoom.

**Frame rate.** The creator asked to aim for 300 fps, a frame of 3.3 ms. The hero's health and hurt flash are written only when they change, so the HUD no longer re-renders every frame, and her derived numbers are computed once per change of her progress rather than every step; the headless development build's CPU fell from 2.66 ms to 2.28 ms a frame. A browser caps its frames at the display's rate, so 300 shows only with vsync off; profile_game on the real GPU measures it.

The creator saw about 20 fps. The headless playtest holds 60 fps in both the old and the new version, but it cannot see what the likeliest cause cost: the old hero was drawn by a second renderer on a canvas of her own, at the device's full pixel ratio with antialiasing, every frame over the world's, which on a phone is three times the resolution. That renderer is gone. The phone's link to the development server is also slower than the built copy.

**The far zones.** The creator asked for more monsters at higher levels, and to check the platform's repository for what could serve. Its shared kit (`packages/assets`) holds rigged, animated monsters under CC0: Quaternius' spider, zombie and demon, and Gobkit's bat. The wilds are now a map of the game's own (`src/maps/wilds.json`), 144 m across where the engine's meadow was 100: the meadow as it was in the middle, and three level clearings down dirt paths, the dusk and skitter hollow to the east among tall trees (Dusk Bats, level 10, and Cave Skitters, 12), the barrow to the west among stones (Barrow Husks, 16), and the ashen flats to the north, bare and rocky (Ash Brutes, 20, and their boss the Ash Warlord, 24, after 10 kills). Outside the clearings the hills and the grass run thinner than the meadow's, so the larger map costs less than its size. A monster with a model (`src/monsters/ModelBody.tsx`) is drawn from the kit's file, toon-lit as she is, sized to its kind: it idles, walks as it roams and runs as it hunts at its pace, swings its attack across the attack's time over the same red ring, flinches and flashes white when hit, glows hot through a wind-up, and, when it falls, plays its death where it fell, lies a moment and sinks. The Warlord casts as the slime bosses do, trembling hot as its fire gathers and swinging as the attack goes off. The far monsters drop more gold and potions, and the rare sets at better odds the further out. `wiki/stats.md` lists every monster. A development copy can start her in any zone with `?at=x,z`.

Proved in the playtest, a level 22 hero arriving in each zone: the bats, skitters, husks and brutes drawn, animated and hunting her, falling to her and lying where they fell with their loot, with no error.

**Bosses in the far zones.** The creator asked for bosses in the new areas. ## Wiki gaps

- A slime reading a trait's store with `getStore` before any entity in the world has the trait crashes the frame loop ("reading 'store'"): the world has no store for it yet. Reading it only once the entity has the trait avoids it.

- A Hud whose own component re-renders can hand its new children a context the engine's bridge lost, such as Motion's config, which crashes every `Button` it mounts ("Cannot destructure property 'reducedMotion'"). Keeping each Hud's component still, with the state in a component inside it, avoids it.
- The engine's Joystick stands in a slot; a stick that floats where the finger lands is the game's own, two circles over the screen.
- CameraControls' `setFocalOffset` had no effect on the rig; the portrait town slides the lens's window with three's `setViewOffset` instead.
- SideCamera takes no lens: a game wanting a narrow one copies it with `useCamera({ fov })`.
- Player has no way to draw nothing but its overlay: a game drawing its own hero hides the placeholder capsule from inside the overlay.
- The UI has no slider: the creator's colour sliders are the page's own range inputs, styled to the house look.
- Custom traits a game adds, such as the monsters' state, are missing from the playtest's dump, so an `until` cannot wait on them; the dump holds the engine's traits alone.
- A row of controls needs `Text as="div"` with a flex `className`: the UI primitives stack a Panel's children in a column and have no row.
- The devtools package's built `dist` bundles its own copies of three, koota and `use-sync-external-store`, whose `require("react")` fails in the browser. A game that links the packages must resolve their source with `@game/source`; the wiki does not say so.
